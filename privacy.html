Discord Infection Bot

Last Updated: October 15, 2026

This Privacy Policy explains what information the Discord Infection Bot (“the Bot”) may process, why it is used, and the choices available to users. Please update this policy if the Bot's actual implementation or hosting practices differ from what is described here.

### 1. Information the Bot May Process

Depending on the features enabled and how the Bot is hosted, it may process or store:

  • Discord user IDs to identify members and associate infection statuses with the correct account.
  • Discord server IDs to keep each server's records separate.
  • Infection records, including infection type (shit or super shit), who assigned it, and when it was recorded, if timestamps are implemented.
  • Command and interaction data needed to respond to commands such as infection, cure, statistics, leaderboard, or prayer commands.
  • Basic technical logs produced by the hosting platform or the Bot, such as errors and diagnostic information.

The Bot is not intended to collect passwords, payment details, or private message content. Do not add claims here unless they accurately reflect the Bot's code and hosting setup.

### 2. How Information Is Used

Information may be used to:

  • Add, remove, and display infection statuses.
  • Count infected members and generate statistics or leaderboards.
  • Determine who assigned an infection, where that feature is enabled.
  • Process commands and interactions.
  • Diagnose errors, maintain security, and keep the Bot running.

### 3. Messages, Mentions, and Replies

If automatic infection features are enabled, the Bot may inspect messages in servers where it has permission to detect mentions or replies and assign an infection status accordingly. The Bot should process only the message information needed for this feature.

Whether message content is available to the Bot depends on its Discord permissions, intents, and implementation. Review the Bot's configuration to ensure this description is accurate.

### 4. Storage and Retention

The Bot may use a database, such as a local SQLite database, to retain infection records between restarts.

Records may remain stored until they are removed by a Bot command, deleted by the Bot owner, or removed as part of database maintenance. Leaving a Discord server does not necessarily delete stored records.
